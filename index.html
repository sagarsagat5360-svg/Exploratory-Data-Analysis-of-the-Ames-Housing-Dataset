THE QUESTION

Which home characteristics move most closely with sale price?

This project describes patterns in historical sales. It uses distribution summaries, Pearson correlations, group medians, and a review of missing values. The results show association, not cause, and they are not current market estimates.

01 · PRICE DISTRIBUTION

Most homes sold between $100,000 and $200,000

The median is below the mean because a smaller set of high-priced homes stretches the upper tail.

910 of 1,460 sales fall in the $100k–$200k range.

02 · NUMERIC ASSOCIATIONS

Overall quality has the strongest numeric correlation

Overall material and finish quality shows the clearest linear association with SalePrice. Above-ground living area is next.

OverallQual: r = 0.791
GrLivArea: r = 0.709

03 · GROUP COMPARISONS

Median prices rise with quality and vary by neighborhood

Choose a grouping to explore the median sale prices. Neighborhoods shown here have at least 20 recorded sales.

Median price ranges from $50,150 for quality 1 (n=2) to $432,390 for quality 10 (n=18). These group differences may reflect other home characteristics as well.

04 · DATA QUALITY

Missing values cluster in optional features

Several amenity fields are blank for most homes. In this dataset, a blank can mean the home does not have that feature.

Read the data description before imputing.

WHAT THE DATA SAYS

Four takeaways

  1. Prices are right-skewed. The median ($163,000) is a better summary of a typical sale than the mean ($180,921).
  2. Quality and usable space stand out. OverallQual and GrLivArea have the largest numeric correlations with price.
  3. Group medians differ. Sale prices vary across quality levels and neighborhoods.
  4. Missingness has meaning. Some blanks represent an absent feature, not an unknown value.

Limits

One city, historical sales from 2006–2010, and uneven group sizes. The analysis is descriptive; correlation does not establish causation.
